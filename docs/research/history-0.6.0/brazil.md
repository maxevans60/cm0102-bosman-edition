# Brazil: competition history 2002 to 2009

Part `brazil` of the slice 11c history research. The rows are in `brazil.tsv` (196 rows: 151 held, 45 not held). All pages were read on 2026-10-17.

## Label convention

Every Brazilian competition in this part uses the calendar year. The label year is the year in which the edition was played. The 3.9.68 rows prove it:

- Brazilian National First Division 2001: Clube Atlético Paranaense, Associação Desportiva São Caetano. This is the 2001 Série A, with its final in December 2001.
- Brazilian Cup 2001: Grêmio Foot-Ball Porto-Alegrense, Sport Club Corinthians Paulista. This is the 2001 Copa do Brasil final (June 2001).
- Brazilian São Paulo State Championship 2001: Sport Club Corinthians Paulista, Botafogo Futebol Clube (SP). This is the 2001 Campeonato Paulista.
- Brazilian Champions Cup 2000 and 2001: Palmeiras over Sport, then Flamengo over São Paulo. These are the 2000 and 2001 Copa dos Campeões.

No competition has two rows per label year. The `part` column is empty in every row.

## The grouped state championships

The data has three competitions with regional names. The 3.9.68 rows from 1995 to 2001 show which state each one holds:

- Brazilian Central State Championship holds the Acre State Championship (Campeonato Acreano). The rows name Rio Branco Football Club, Independência Futebol Clube and Associação Desportiva Vasco da Gama, all clubs of Rio Branco, Acre. The 2001 row (Vasco da Gama, Rio Branco) is the real 2001 Acreano result.
- Brazilian North State Championship holds the Alagoas State Championship (Campeonato Alagoano). The rows name CSA, CRB, ASA and Miguelense. The 2000 and 2001 rows (ASA over CSA) are the real Alagoano results.
- Brazilian Northeast State Championship holds the Amazonas State Championship (Campeonato Amazonense). The rows name Nacional, São Raimundo, Rio Negro and Clíper, all of Manaus. The 2001 row (Rio Negro, Nacional) is the real 2001 Amazonense result.

I researched these three as Acre, Alagoas and Amazonas. The databases from September 2009 on use the same three names for other states (see below).

## Competitions that ended

- Brazilian Taça Brazil: the Taça Brasil was last played in 1968. All eight label years are `not held`.
- Brazilian Champions Cup (Copa dos Campeões): last edition 2002, abolished after it.
- Brazilian Central Regional Cup (Copa Centro-Oeste) and Brazilian North Regional Cup (Copa Norte): last edition 2002. Both returned only in 2026.
- Brazilian Northeast Regional Cup (Copa do Nordeste): held 2002 and 2003, not held 2004 to 2009, back in 2010.
- Brazilian South-Minas Regional Cup (Copa Sul-Minas): last edition 2002. The range for this competition is 2002 to 2005.
- Brazilian Rio-São Paulo Tournement (Torneio Rio-São Paulo): last edition 2002.

## The 2002 split championships

In 2002 the big clubs of three states played the interstate cups, and their federations ran the state championship without them:

- Minas Gerais 2002: the official champion is Caldense, runner-up Ipatinga. Cruzeiro won a separate Supercampeonato Mineiro, which the Federação Mineira does not recognise.
- Paraná 2002: the official champion is Iraty, runner-up Grêmio Maringá. Atlético Paranaense won a separate Supercampeonato Paranaense over Paraná Clube.
- São Paulo 2002: the official champion is Ituano, runner-up União São João. São Paulo won the separate Supercampeonato Paulista over Ituano in May 2002.

The rows hold the official championship. If the game should show the big-club competition instead, the alternatives are in `notes`.

## Lower divisions

- Brazilian Minas Gerais Lower Division is the Módulo II, the second level of the Campeonato Mineiro.
- Brazilian Rio de Janeiro Lower Division is the second level of the Campeonato Carioca. Its name changed (Série A2, Segunda Divisão from 2005, Série B from 2009), but the level is the same.

## Points of doubt

- Santa Catarina 2002 and 2003 runners-up. The prose of the pt.wikipedia page names Caxias as 2002 runner-up. The same page lists Criciúma as runner-up in 2002, and en.wikipedia gives Criciúma 2002 and Caxias 2003. I used Criciúma for 2002 and Caxias for 2003.
- Amazonas 2009 runner-up. The pt.wikipedia prose says Fast Clube were runners-up four years in a row, 2006 to 2009. Its table and the RSSSF Brasil page (America beat Nacional 3-0 in the second leg, 28 June 2009) give Nacional. I used Nacional.
- Paraná 2006 runner-up ADAP (Associação Desportiva Atlética do Paraná). I matched it to ADAP Galo Maringá FC. This is an inference: the club later moved to Maringá under that name.
- Paulista Futebol Clube (São Paulo 2004 runner-up, 2005 Copa do Brasil winner) is the club that the 3.9.68 data holds as Etti Jundiaí Futebol Clube. The rows use the later name, which is in the databases from September 2005 on.
- Ended month unknown (written `unknown`): Acre 2009 (the archived RSSSF Brasil page stops at the first stage), Santa Catarina 2002 and 2003 (no page with dates found), Rio de Janeiro Lower Division 2008 and 2009 (no archived page).
- Ended month is the month of the final or of the last league match day. Relegation play-offs after the final are not counted (Bahia 2005, São Paulo 2003).
- Most RSSSF Brasil pages were read through web.archive.org copies, because the live site refused connections on the day. The source URLs are the archive URLs.

## Clubs not in the data

- ADESG (Associação Desportiva Senador Guiomard): Acre runner-up 2005, champion 2006.
- Grêmio Coariense (Grêmio Atlético Coariense): Amazonas runner-up 2004, champion 2005.
