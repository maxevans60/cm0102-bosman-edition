# Second-source check: Italy (part `ita`, slice 5)

Checked file: `ita.tsv` (459 rows). Check file: `ita.check.tsv`, one line for each filled fact field of each row (2,112 lines). Fields that hold `n/a` were not checked. I did not change the first agent's files.

## Method

1. I read every second-source page on 2026-10-16 with `curl` and grep, or with `pdftotext` for FIGC PDFs. No number in the check comes from a summary model. Search results only pointed me to pages, and I cite only pages that I read.
2. Second sources come from publishers other than Wikipedia, which is the first source of most rows:
   - RSSSF season pages (`ital02.html` to `ital09.html`, `ital2010.html` to `ital2026.html`, and the lower-level pages `ital2-05` to `ital4-09`) for final tables, promotion, relegation, play-off and play-out rules, Coppa Italia rounds, and the super cup list.
   - Transfermarkt league tables (Serie A `IT1`, Serie B `IT2`, C1 and Serie C `IT3A` to `IT3C`, C2 `IT4X` to `IT4Z`, Serie D `IT4A` to `IT4I`) for club counts, and its cup pages (`CIT`, `CILP`, `SCIC`, `SCC2`, `IT3P`, `IT3O`, `IT4W`) for rounds, legs, entrants, play-offs and play-outs. Where Transfermarkt has no season, its page falls back to the current season. I did not count such pages as evidence.
   - FIGC communiqués: C.U. 44/A and 45/A (Aug 2017, substitutes and bench), 194/A (June 2004, NOIF art. 40), 269/A (Apr 2025) and 245/A (May 2026, squad lists), 25/A (July 2026, Serie B play-offs and play-outs). An LND circular quotes C.U. 295/A (Serie D bench 2021/22).
   - News pages: LaPresse, Calcio e Finanza, Corriere dello Sport, TuttoMercatoWeb, Calciomercato, La Sicilia, Sky Sport, Il Post, fantacalcio.it, Fantamaster, Fantacalcio-Online, Virgilio, AGI, ESPN, Goal. Where the first agent used one of these publishers for a field, I used a different publisher for that field.
3. Verdicts: `agree`, `disagree`, `no second source`, `found`. A `found` line gives a value for a field that the first file marks `unknown`.
4. Where a Transfermarkt final table has fewer clubs than the row because a club was excluded during the season, I marked `agree` and named the club in the note.

## Main findings for Max

- Serie B play-off gap. From 2017/18 the 3rd is promoted directly only with a lead of more than 14 points (FIGC C.U. 25/A for 2026/27; Calcio e Finanza for the 2017 reform). The first file gives "more than 9" for every season from 2013/14. For 2013/14 to 2016/17 the 9-point rule agrees, but a 14-point "play-off perimeter" also limited the field to 2 to 6 clubs.
- Serie B play-out gap. RSSSF gives "less than 6 points" for 2004/05 to 2006/07 and "less than 4 points" for 2009/10 and 2010/11. The first file gives "not held if over 4" for these seasons. From 2020/21 the "over 4" rule agrees with three other publishers and with FIGC C.U. 25/A for 2026/27.
- Serie A squad list. The free players are under-22 in 2020/21 and 2022/23 to 2024/25, and under-23 from 2025/26. The first file gives under-21 from 2016/17 on.
- Prima Divisione 2013/14 play-off had 16 clubs with one-leg quarter-finals (Transfermarkt).
- Seconda Divisione 2010/11: RSSSF and Transfermarkt show one play-out between 15th and 16th, with a 6-point gap rule. The first file gives last plus two play-out losers.
- Serie C 2019/20 used five substitutes at the 2020 restart. Coppa Italia Serie C 2019/20 had a one-match final.
- Supercoppa Italiana: the 2023/24 final was on 22 January 2024. The 18 January date was the first semi-final. The 2026/27 match is at San Siro on 22 December 2026, not in Saudi Arabia.
- Non-EU rule 2001/02: FIGC C.U. 194/A of 25 June 2004 still prints NOIF art. 40.7 as unchanged. That text allows a Serie A club at most five non-EU players from foreign federations, with three in the match list, and a Serie B club one. The first file says the quota was abolished in 2000/01. Annual derogations may explain the difference, and I could not read one. Max decides.
- Transfer windows: summer 2023 closed on 1 September. Summer 2026 opened on 29 June. Winter 2024/25 closes on 2 February in the June 2024 announcement (a Sunday). The March 2018 plan closed summer 2018 on 18 August.

## Unknowns that I looked for

- The non-EU signing quota for 2003/04 to 2007/08: not found. A FIGC court decision says C.U. 179/A of 14 May 2004 let Serie A clubs sign a non-EU player in 2004/05 only to replace one who had left. I could not open that page, so I give it no verdict.
- Supercoppa di Lega di Seconda Divisione: found on Transfermarkt. It had three clubs in a triangular from 2005/06 to 2010/11 and two clubs over two legs from 2011/12 to 2013/14.
- Coppa Italia Serie C round format: found for 2010/11, 2011/12, 2013/14, 2014/15 and 2015/16 (group stage of three-club groups, then knockout rounds). Entrants for 2001/02 to 2003/04: not found.
- Bench sizes: found for 2017/18 to 2021/22 for Coppa Italia, Supercoppa Italiana (12, FIGC C.U. 44/A) and Supercoppa di Serie C 2017/18 (12). Found for Serie D in 2017/18 (9 reserves, 5 substitutes) and 2021/22 (9 reserves).
- Serie C squad list 2020/21: found (22 players with at most 8 loans, plus one slot for a player born in 2001 or later).
- Winter window 2026/27: found (2 January to 1 February 2027).
- Still unknown: Serie B bench from 2022/23, third-tier bench before 2017/18, the C1 and C2 registration rules, Serie D relegation numbers, transfer windows before 2006/07.
